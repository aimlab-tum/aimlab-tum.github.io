Our group develops AI methods for medicine and healthcare along two complementary directions: advancing large language models and AI agents for medical applications, and building biomedical world models to understand biological systems and predict their responses to interventions.

**LLMs and agents for medicine and healthcare.** We investigate how large language models and multimodal models can support medical reasoning, patient interaction, and clinical workflows. Our research focuses on systems that integrate medical images, clinical records, and biomedical knowledge, gather relevant evidence, and use specialised tools to complete complex tasks. We explore reinforcement learning, reasoning, and agent collaboration to improve their capabilities, with applications including medical dialogue, imaging analysis, and clinical decision support. We also develop benchmarks and auditing methods to assess their reliability in realistic healthcare settings.

**Biomedical world models.** Our long-term goal is to build models that capture how biological systems evolve and respond to interventions. We aim to combine multimodal biomedical data, biological knowledge, and mechanistic and learned models to connect processes across cells, organs, and patients. This includes exploring digital organisms in which agents coordinate specialised models for simulation and scientific investigation. Potential applications include studying disease progression, comparing candidate interventions, and guiding experiments. A central research question is how to establish when these predictions are sufficiently supported by evidence for their intended use.

Across both directions, we emphasise rigorous evaluation, uncertainty, and clinical relevance. Our aim is to develop AI systems that support clinicians and researchers, with capabilities and limitations that can be clearly assessed.
